# School Civil Rights Investigations and Complaints Under Trump’s Education Department 

ProPublica sued the federal government for records that show which schools are facing civil rights investigations — data that was public before Trump’s second term.

This page shares the data we received, which we used for our October 2026 story “[Sexual Assaults on Campus No Longer Scrutinized as Closely Under Trump’s Education Department](https://www.propublica.org/article/civil-rights-office-education-department-investigations).” 

We found that the civil rights arm of the U.S. Department of Education has dramatically scaled back investigations involving schools and colleges since the second Trump administration began, even as complaints continue to pour in.

ProPublica’s analysis shows how severely the department’s Office for Civil Rights has retracted from its mission, launching an average of about 100 new investigations a month — 1,839 total from Inauguration Day in 2025 through July 2026\. The OCR launched an average of 310 new investigations each month in 2024 during the Biden administration, according to data previously obtained by ProPublica.

Under President Donald Trump, the OCR abandoned the [public-facing list of schools it was investigating and the reasons for those investigations](https://ocrcas.ed.gov/open-investigations?field_ois_state=All&field_ois_discrimination_statute=All&field_ois_type_of_discrimination=727&items_per_page=20&field_ois_institution=&field_ois_institution_type=All&field_open_investigation_date_1=2024-01-01%2000%3A00%3A00&field_open_investigation_date_2=2025-01-01%2023%3A59%3A59&field_open_investigation_date=&field_open_investigation_date_3=&field_open_investigation_dat_1=2026-10-15%2000%3A00%3A00&field_open_investigation_dat_2=2026-10-15%2023%3A59%3A59&page=2), including claims of discrimination based on race, sex and disability. [ProPublica sued the department](https://www.propublica.org/article/education-department-civil-rights-office-foia-lawsuit) to obtain internal case data. The government subsequently provided records of the OCR’s actions in the first 18 months of Trump’s return to power, and we are publishing the data so the public can search the cases.

## What We Received

In response to our lawsuit, the Department of Education provided the following to ProPublica:  
 

* A list of complaints, provided to ProPublica in August 2026: [ocr\_case\_actions.xlsx](https://github.com/propublica/ocr-investigations-data/blob/b7188377280150ec8634788117a5765f2c9322ad/ocr_case_actions.xlsx). This included:  
  * Complaints received by the OCR from Jan. 20, 2025, through May 26, 2026, and assigned a docket number.  
  * Complaints dismissed or closed by the OCR from Jan. 20, 2025, through July 2026, regardless of when the case was received.  
  * Complaints in response to which the OCR under Trump officially launched an investigation, regardless of when the complaint was received.  
* A list of investigation notices sent to institutions, provided to ProPublica in June 2026: [ocr\_investigation\_notifications.xlsx](https://github.com/propublica/ocr-investigations-data/blob/b7188377280150ec8634788117a5765f2c9322ad/ocr_investigation_notifications.xlsx)  
  * When the OCR initiates an investigation — either as the result of a complaint or internally through a “directed investigation” — it notifies the target of the investigation. This file is a record of those notifications.  
  * This includes notices sent from Jan. 20, 2025, through April 9, 2026\. 

## How We Analyzed the Data

We published an [interactive table](https://www.propublica.org/article/civil-rights-office-education-department-investigations) that combines the two files received. The data from that table is available for download here: [ocr\_combined\_activity\_propublica.csv](https://github.com/propublica/ocr-investigations-data/blob/main/ocr_combined_activity_propublica.csv).

It includes investigations launched by the second Trump administration, complaints the administration dismissed without investigating (some of which were filed years earlier) and complaints filed during the Trump administration that the office has not yet decided to investigate. 

The OCR uses “case opened” to record when complaints were received by the agency. “Investigation start date” indicates the date the agency initiated an investigation.

Some cases do not stem from a complaint but are initiated by the agency. In those cases, “case open” indicates when the case was launched internally. Most Trump administration investigations started with a complaint, but if an investigation was initiated internally rather than through complaint, it appeared in only the investigation notices file. As a result, the combined table does not include internally initiated cases after April 9\.

The two files list different issue descriptions to refer to the same cases, and we utilized issue descriptions from both datasets in our analysis. For example, “sexual violence” appears only in the complaint file descriptions, while “discrimination against males” appears only in the investigation notices file. If a case was present in both, the combined table displays the case information from the complaint file.
